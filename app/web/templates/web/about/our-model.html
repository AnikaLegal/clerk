Our model: how a small team
helps hundreds of renters

Renters face rising costs, unsafe homes and a legal system that is hard to navigate alone, and traditional services cannot meet the scale of that need. So we built a different way of working.

Who works on a case

Every case is assigned a law student paralegal, who is the renter's point of contact throughout. Their work is supervised by a lawyer, who guides them and signs off on all legal advice. Experienced paralegals triage new enquiries and provide support, and our Principal Lawyer oversees the whole practice.

This structure is how we can help more people. One full-time lawyer supervising students and secondees can support up to 300 renters a year. In 2024-25, four staff, 136 law students, 90 volunteers and a group of lawyers seconded from law firms delivered 508 legal services to 333 renters.

Our university partnerships

Universities and education providers play a critical role in sustaining our service model. Law schools are increasingly required to provide authentic workplace learning, and we deliver it at scale, at low cost, and with strong student outcomes. In return, our education partners provide the funding and the recurring student capacity that let us grow.

This is our only commercial activity, and it was under a tenth of our income in 2024-25. Every dollar of it goes back into the free service, helping to pay for the lawyers who supervise those students, and none of it is distributed as profit. It supports the mission directly rather than sitting alongside it: the students a university pays us to teach are the students who, supervised by our lawyers, get a renter's home repaired or their bond returned.

A model where everyone wins

Anika’s student-powered service model creates shared value – every participant benefits in a meaningful way:

  • Renters receive free, high-quality support when they need it most.
  • Students gain practical legal experience that enhances their employability.
  • Universities improve law student learning outcomes with minimal additional investment.
  • The community legal sector gains a scalable workforce pipeline and dramatically increased capacity.

This design unlocks value that already exists in the system — tens of thousands of law students seeking experience and millions of Australians needing legal help — and channels it into an efficient, high-impact service model.

How we are funded

The service is funded mostly by philanthropic grants, with corporate and community donations making up most of the rest. Only a small proportion is earned income, from our university partnerships. On top of that, a large amount of the work is done by volunteers, seconded lawyers and pro bono partners, whose time and expertise are worth a substantial share of what the service runs on.

For the full figures, see our independently reviewed financial report for the year to 30 June 2025. Anika Legal Ltd is a registered charity, listed on the ACNC charity register.
